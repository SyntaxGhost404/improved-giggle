# সুন্দরবন: যে নৌকা ডোবেনি

**The Sundarbans: The Boat That Never Sank**

A direct sequel to the grounded Raja Chapitala investigation of **2026**. Moin narrates; Dr. Nasir Uddin leads. Proposed length: 24 chapters, approximately 55,000–70,000 words. Research checked on 13 September 2026. All new boats, businesses, crimes, and non-family characters below are fictional.

**The premise in one paragraph**

A winter holiday aboard a family acquaintance’s river vessel becomes an investigation when two men arrive in a small boat, one injured, claiming their working launch has sunk and its skipper has disappeared. Nasir treats the injured man but refuses to certify a cause he did not witness. The launch is subsequently glimpsed afloat. Recovering its real history exposes a planned theft and a killing that the survivors have concealed. The four visitors contribute observations, voluntarily shared records, and support for a frightened witness; the crew navigate and the authorities investigate. Separately, two encounters with an unseen presence remain unresolved. Neither supplies evidence against a human being.

The emotional question is: **What happens when the person you are obliged to help may have harmed someone else?**

**2. The geographical and legal frame**

Use the **Bangladesh Sundarbans**, reached through **Khulna and Mongla**, with the **Pashur/Pasur river corridor and the northern/eastern forest approaches** as the real geographical frame. Karamjal and Harbaria provide recognisable visitor locations. The Forest Department lists both on its [official Sundarbans tourism portal](https://sundarbantourism.bforest.gov.bd/en).

The forest is a network of tidal waterways, mangrove islands, and mudflats, rather than a continuous stretch of land through which the boys can simply walk. That geographical fact should control the suspense: one bend removes a sightline; exposed mud becomes covered; a usable landing changes with water level. [UNESCO’s Sundarbans description](https://whc.unesco.org/en/list/798/) supports this physical setting. Its World Heritage property and the wider Sundarbans Reserve Forest are not interchangeable boundaries.

**Real geography does not authorise an invented itinerary.** The following is a fiction-planning framework, not a navigation chart or a claim that every nearby creek is open.

Do not assign invented coordinates to those three fictional places. In the novel’s internal map, the skipper’s approved itinerary explicitly includes the side-channel anchorage and any tender access. The shore repair pontoon sits outside the reserve on a lawful commercial waterfront in the Mongla approach. Its precise placement is fictional.

For drafting, the outward plan is Mongla, the Karamjal/Pashur approach, a quiet approved anchorage, and a Harbaria excursion, followed by slow exploration of additional approved waterways. The rescue interrupts that plan on the second day. The vessel returns towards an authorised station and onward medical access instead of continuing farther into the forest. It stays in that northern area for the rest of the case. Do not suddenly add Katka, Kochikhali, Hiron Point, Dublar Char, or the western border to fill six days: this is a slow private visit, not a checklist cruise.

**How the private vessel works**

The host owns a small fleet. The visitors live on his **purpose-fitted cabin vessel, MV Joarrekha (জোয়াররেখা)**. It already has the registration, survey/fitness status, accommodation, and operator arrangements needed for its intended visitor service. His working fishing craft are separate. He uses the ordinary approval process for this particular group, route, and overnight stops.

The story stipulates that approvals have been completed before departure. This is a fictional logistical fact, not a researched promise that an application will always be approved in several days. A privately owned fishing boat carrying friends cannot simply treat its fishing papers as blanket tourist permission. GSETS states that entering the protected forest and staying overnight aboard require Forest Department permission, usually arranged by the operator. [Fees and permits](https://greatersundarbans.org/fees-permits/).

Assign an experienced skipper, engineer, cook, deck crew, local nature guide, and **two authorised forest escorts** to this fictional departure. Their presence is established at boarding. The exact complement required for a real 2027 trip would depend on its approval. The family do not borrow the escorts’ powers or direct them as a private police force.

The host’s invitation to cast nets and drop lines is fulfilled on an authorised fishing reach outside sanctuary/no-take waters, under his licensed crew’s supervision and within whatever guest participation is allowed. Place the principal fishing scene before the protected forest excursion. In protected reaches, the visitors watch rather than fish. Do not turn an empty channel into presumed permission. The conservation context is explained in [GSETS’ dolphin article](https://greatersundarbans.org/dolphins-in-the-sundarbans/); its broad list should not be used as a boundary map.

A fish caught legally that morning can be cooked aboard in another permitted location. This preserves the requested hospitality without pretending that every meal must have been caught beside the night’s anchorage.

The Pashur has working river traffic. Quietness comes from timing, scale, and a bend screened by mangroves—not from asserting that no one uses the region. Some stretches have weak phone reception; others allow ordinary calls. Show one successful call early so the later gaps do not look like a conveniently broken universe.

**Season and texture**

Write short December afternoons, occasional morning haze or fog, cool air over the water, wet ropes, mud on landing steps, and a warmer galley. These are proposed scene conditions, not a forecast for December 2027. Actual tide times, navigable depth, and safe anchorages must come from the skipper’s local information for the date; there is no universal “high tide at midnight” rule.

Use ordinary interruptions: diesel vibration through a bunk, a kettle lid chattering, a dolphin surfacing too briefly for Oli’s camera, birds visible before their names are known, the cook complaining that everyone has abandoned breakfast. The forest need not fall completely silent whenever something sinister happens. No guaranteed tiger encounter is necessary.

Moin’s argument is about shared discovery: Oli and Fakhruddin have already been to Bandarban; Moin and Oli have never seen the Sundarbans, and Fakhruddin barely has. **5. The complete human truth: author-only information**

The fictional working launch **MS Rahima-3** belongs to Mahbub. Introduce its skipper **Babul**, crewman **Tuhin, 20**, and contract boatman **Salek, 36**, while they are still ordinary people at work. Babul should be alive on the page before he becomes a missing person. He corrects Oli’s attempt to handle a fishing line and checks whether Tuhin has eaten. Give him a future concern—a repair he wants completed before the next working trip—rather than a speech predicting death.

Mahbub’s fleet dispatcher **Bashar, 41**, has arranged to sell Rahima-3 without the owner’s consent. He has personal debts and an opportunity: Mahbub expects to spend several days away aboard Joarrekha. Bashar knows the fleet’s paperwork and routines but does not own its vessels. He prepares false owner-authorisation papers and negotiates with **Hatem**, an independent repair-yard proprietor who can introduce a buyer. A small initial payment has already been made; the larger payment awaits inspection and completed ownership checks.

This is a modest, specific crime opportunity, not an international syndicate. Bashar expects to misdirect the owner with a reported river loss while the launch is taken to a repair berth for alteration and sale. The scheme is vulnerable to owner verification; he hopes to complete it before that happens. Do not write him as having a foolproof plan.

On the morning of 20 December, Bashar and Salek divert a routine working movement. Babul discovers that the instruction involves an unauthorised disposal of the boat and refuses. Salek strikes him during the struggle; Bashar forces him overboard. Tuhin witnesses the attack. Babul dies, and the culprits do not attempt an honest rescue. Salek’s injury is real, sustained when Babul resisted.

They threaten Tuhin with harm and retaliation against his family if he contradicts them. They deliver Rahima-3 afloat to the repair berth around midday. Salek then takes the smaller boat, with Tuhin, to Joarrekha and reports a sinking that supposedly happened later that afternoon. The yard has already taken custody of the launch and kept it there throughout that alleged accident window. This timing, subsequently checked against staff accounts and the original handling records, rules out an innocent overnight salvage explanation. Bashar claims to have been attending shore business and acts as the helpful dispatcher of search information.

Bashar’s camera-recorded additional visit occurs during the earlier working movements, before the assault. His claim is that he left after the initial morning visit and did not return until the rescue. The camera disproves that broad account; it does not timestamp the killing. Keep all boat movements within the northern working corridor and allow real travel intervals. The story does not require two distant locations to be reached in minutes.

Salek adds that Babul seemed to hear a familiar voice from the trees just before the supposed accident. He borrows language from nervous talk about the forest. **That part of his account is a human lie.** It is distinct from the two events the family genuinely experience.

Nasir’s presence was not part of the original scheme. When Salek needs treatment, a familiar owner’s boat carrying a surgeon is useful. The criminals also recognise that a respected doctor’s note might lend credibility to their story. Nasir denies them that advantage by recording only what he observed and what the patient reported.

Nasir then insists on shore assessment. The main vessel turns back earlier than the thieves expected, while a station-coordinated search continues separately. The returning vessel is consequently in the northern approach when Rahima-3 is shifted between berths within the yard. **The protagonists encounter the next clue because of a responsible decision, not because a supernatural voice directs them to it.**

Bashar later tries to regain control over Tuhin and retrieve company papers before independent accounts can be compared. He still needs the sale’s balance and believes he can manage an ownership dispute through his position; he does not know that Tuhin has just disclosed the attack. Returning is a desperate misjudgement, not a calculated escape plan. The attempt fails in front of people already alerted to the risk. Tuhin gives a voluntary statement. The recovered launch, original documents, yard evidence, payment trail, medical records, and later forensic work corroborate his account.

The human case ends with responsibility established for the killing, intimidation and attempted disposal of the launch. Formal findings arrive later than the holiday. The final narration may refer to the completed case; it must not turn two days of enquiries into an instant conviction.

**6. The two unexplained events—and the boundary around them**

Islamic belief in jinn is the religious foundation. The Qur’an describes their creation and distinguishes morally different groups among them; it does not identify a particular Sundarbans creek as inhabited by jinn. [Qur’an 55:15](https://quran.com/en/ar-rahman/15), [72:11](https://quran.com/en/al-jinn/11).

Specific voice imitation and the appearance below are **fictional manifestations**, not presented as authenticated local incidents or scriptural descriptions of mangrove behaviour. Regional Bonbibi traditions belong to the Sundarbans’ diverse cultural life; they should not be flattened into Islamic doctrine or renamed a jinn cult. [Sahapedia’s account of Bonbibi performance traditions](https://www.sahapedia.org/bonbibi-r-palagaan-tradition-history-and-performance) provides that distinction.

In the private author’s model, an actual nonhuman presence exists independently of the crime. The characters never establish its identity, intentions, or mechanism. The book does not eventually disclose a speaker, accomplice, hallucination, dream, or electronic trick behind these two encounters. At the same time, Moin does not claim his experience constitutes scientific proof of a named entity.

| Event | What is directly experienced | What remains unresolved |

Neither encounter mentions Babul, a document, a boat number, a destination, or a danger that changes the investigation’s outcome. The presence does not punish the guilty or protect the innocent on cue.

Ahsanul Mama’s response is calm: seek Allah’s protection, stay together, do not follow an unseen caller, and report concrete danger. He can recall the supplication associated with stopping at a place, without conducting a telephone exorcism. [Sahih Muslim 2708a](https://sunnah.com/muslim:2708a) is a relevant religious source. **8. The 24-chapter sequence**

**Chapter 1 — Thirty-two districts (12–13 December)**

Open at home after Fakhruddin’s arrival. Allow jet lag, unpacking and familiar interruptions before the destination debate. His list of districts prompts Moin’s Sundarbans suggestion; Oli argues for Bandarban; Moin makes the mutual-discovery argument. Their previous Cumilla adventure appears in two or three teasing remarks, not an exposition lecture. They are not famous detectives now.

End when Oli says they should ask Choto Mama to come. The hook is family anticipation rather than an anonymous threat.

**Chapter 2 — The patient in the story (13 December)**

The video call preserves Mama’s affectionate greeting, his inability to leave religious duties, and the fisherman’s repeated invitation. Fresh fish cooked aboard sells the plan before anyone mentions mysterious channels. Fakhruddin’s joke about Nasir prompts the patient/shutki revelation.

Briefly clarify the two-coast business connection. Let Oli interrupt the explanation with disbelief, while Moin is already imagining sleeping over water. Mama undertakes to ask the host; he does not promise access to any forbidden part of the forest.

**Chapter 3 — “Impossible” (14–17 December)**

Mama calls back: Mahbub is delighted; Nasir cannot come. The group accepts the disappointment. Preparations reveal character through ordinary decisions: Oli packs two cameras; Fakhruddin asks about the boat’s sleeping arrangements; Moin keeps returning to the train journey.

Privately, in the author’s chronology, Nasir’s leave becomes possible. Moin does not narrate this yet. The later explanation is short and verifiable: approved leave, duties handed over, a separate road journey, Mama and the host keeping the surprise.

**Chapter 4 — A train after thirteen years (18 December)**

Give the journey room: the platform, finding their compartment, the movement unlike a metro, tea held carefully, conversation dissolving as people sleep. Do not invent mountains outside a southwestern train window or rely on an unverified 2027 departure time.

Moin remembers being much smaller on his last conventional train. Fakhruddin’s presence makes that memory personal. They reach Khulna and stay overnight rather than performing an implausibly seamless midnight transfer into a secluded forest channel.

**Chapter 5 — The man eating fish (19 December)**

At Mongla, Mahbub’s welcome is warm and specific. He knows the family through the darbar and knows Nasir through treatment and repeated shop visits. Then comes the curly-haired figure with the fish plate.

Keep Nasir’s reveal exactly casual. He speaks to Oli and Fakhruddin as though they are late for an ordinary meal. Moin’s outrage lasts longer than Nasir’s explanation. The surprise establishes both his humour and the ease with which the younger men accept him as leader.

**Chapter 6 — Learning the boat (19 December)**

Establish Joarrekha’s layout through use: cabins, galley, side boarding point, upper passage, engine access, crew space and toilets. Introduce Jalal, Shamima and the escorts; show proper life jackets and the skipper deciding movements.

Before the forest leg, the host demonstrates permitted fishing with his working crew. Introduce Babul, Tuhin and Salek aboard Rahima-3, and Bashar as the busy dispatcher. Oli’s imperfect cast gives Babul an ordinary, humorous scene. Mahbub points out an old repair on Rahima-3’s forward rail while discussing its maintenance. That detail must seem like boat talk, not an announced clue.

Oli mounts and tests the wildlife camera with permission. The boat enters on the approved itinerary. The first strange knocking sound is explained immediately: the engineer shows a loose wooden fitting touching the hull. Reader and narrator learn that an unsettling noise deserves inspection.

**Chapter 7 — Nasir’s voice (19 December night)**

At the authorised quiet anchorage, Moin and Oli stand with Nasir near the rail. A voice from the dark bank calls Moin in Nasir’s familiar tone. Nasir’s face is visible beside them; he has not spoken.

Nobody jumps into a tender. Jalal and an escort help check from aboard; nothing is located. The family tell the skipper what they heard. They do not pretend that a torch sweep has proved the entire bank empty. Where reception permits, Mama offers calm religious counsel. No visitor receives a special revelation.

End with the discomfort of continuing an ordinary conversation beside someone whose voice they have just heard elsewhere.

**Chapter 8 — A good morning (20 December morning)**

Give the characters a genuine holiday morning after the disturbance: breakfast, a permitted forest walk with the guide and escort, birdwatching, an unsuccessful attempt at a good phone photograph. It should be possible to believe the story might still become a happy travel memoir.

The changing tide affects the landing steps. Jalal explains that familiarity with one river is not mastery of another. Fakhruddin admits how little he remembers from his DU trip. This prepares the boys to listen to local expertise rather than derive routes from phone maps.

**Chapter 9 — Two men, one missing boat (20 December afternoon)**

During the return to an approved mooring, a small boat approaches carrying Salek and Tuhin. Salek has a genuine injury; Tuhin is shaken. They say Rahima-3 has sunk and Babul is missing. Salek mentions a voice from the trees and an accident.

Nasir starts with the patient. The skipper and escorts initiate contact for the missing man and medical transfer. Moin helps with a clean cloth or fetches a bag; he does not take over an examination. Tuhin begins “নৌকাটা…” and stops when Salek looks at him. This is an observation, not proof of coercion.

The holiday ends because someone needs help.

**Chapter 10 — What the doctor can sign (20 December afternoon/evening)**

Bashar arrives as the apparently useful dispatcher and helps describe the alleged loss. He asks whether Nasir can provide a note confirming the boat accident. Nasir records the injury, assessment and reported history, but says he cannot certify a sinking he did not witness.

The request is not automatically criminal: survivors may reasonably want documentation. Its meaning changes only after later evidence. Nasir insists on shore assessment; the skipper implements the practical transfer. The main vessel heads towards station-side and shore support while the missing-person search is separately coordinated. Darkness, tide and available craft govern movements; do not promise a five-minute rescue from every creek.

**Chapter 11 — Three versions of the afternoon (21 December morning)**

The family are now staying aboard near the northern approach while the immediate work continues. Mahbub voluntarily explains the working launch’s planned movements. Salek’s account, Bashar’s account, and what the family saw do not fit perfectly.

Fakhruddin lists each claim with its speaker. Moin remembers the earlier Cumilla lesson: a confident name or cause can be added to an uncertain observation. Nasir cautions that injuries, confusion and fear can also produce inconsistent accounts. They have grounds to preserve details, not grounds to announce a murderer.

**Chapter 12 — The additional visit (21 December morning)**

Oli retrieves the camera card during ordinary equipment checks, now that urgent care has been handed over. One clip shows Bashar’s additional boarding visit on the preceding morning, contrary to his broad claim that he had left after his first visit and had not returned until the rescue.

They compare clocks and recognise the clip’s narrow field. Asked about it by Mahbub, Bashar supplies a new ordinary explanation—a forgotten item or an additional instruction. That may explain the visit; it does not erase the changed account. The original footage and discrepancy go to the responsible investigator. The family do not secretly place more cameras around people’s cabins.

**Chapter 13 — The boat in the photograph (21 December midday)**

Joarrekha’s shortened route puts the group near a lawful commercial repair berth. Moin takes a casual phone photograph of working boats. He notices a forward-rail repair resembling the one Mahbub discussed on Rahima-3.

Oli initially wants to make an identification immediately. Moin stops him: a similar repair is not a boat’s identity. They show Mahbub, who has a legitimate reason to enquire about his missing property. The location is accessible from an ordinary landing; there is no trespass into an abandoned forest compound.

The chapter ends with Hatem, the yard proprietor, saying the boat was delivered for work—not recovered from a sinking.

**Chapter 14 — Hatem’s refusal (21 December afternoon)**

Hatem does not owe three visitors access to a client’s boat or records. His initial refusal makes him a plausible suspect and demonstrates the limit of amateur curiosity. Nasir’s job title does not change that.

Mahbub shows his own documentation and asks Hatem to verify the ownership position with the investigating officer. Hatem becomes cooperative when he sees a concrete dispute, producing his own order record and the authorisation supplied to him. He retains his rights and copies; the group do not confiscate anything.

The launch is identified through actual vessel/engine identifiers and owner records under proper access. Similar paint or a repaired rail only led to the enquiry. Hatem’s staff and handling records place the launch in the yard before, and continuously through, the afternoon when Salek said it sank. The investigators check that account. It cannot be explained away as a boat salvaged afterwards.

**Chapter 15 — A sale before an accident (21 December late afternoon)**

The yard records show that the sale/refit arrangement began before the alleged loss. Mahbub says he authorised no sale. Bashar supplied the papers; Salek was involved in delivery. The full originals, receipt/payment records and communications become matters for the investigators to check.

Fakhruddin’s useful deduction is chronological: a planned transaction cannot have been invented merely as an innocent response to yesterday’s wreck. However, an unauthorised sale does not by itself prove how Babul died. Nasir insists that those are separate questions.

Hatem is cleared as a knowing participant when his documented insistence on ownership checks, his retained records, and subsequent investigation support his account. His initial brusqueness remains ordinary business caution.

**Chapter 16 — Mama knows one thing (21 December evening)**

The group briefly wonder whether the host’s invitation itself was a trap. Mama can resolve only what he personally knows: Mahbub invited the family long before this specific trip, and Nasir’s attendance was arranged after his initial refusal. That removes an unsupported interpretation of the family coincidence; it does not clear every employee.

When asked about the borrowed voice, Mama repeats that belief in jinn does not establish the source of a particular sound. He declines to attach a supernatural explanation to Babul’s disappearance. He advises them to keep Nasir, the skipper and the responsible officials informed.

No new contact, secret map or folklore password comes through the phone.

**Chapter 17 — Tuhin chooses to speak (22 December morning)**

After appropriate shore assessment and discharge, Tuhin returns with an escort/known responsible adult to collect belongings and speak to Mahbub aboard. He is medically fit to do so; a seriously injured patient is not kept on the holiday vessel for plot convenience.

Nasir notices that he asks who is listening before answering ordinary questions. Privacy had already been offered during initial care and on shore; fear prevented a disclosure then. What changes is that Tuhin now knows the launch has been found, so his word will not stand alone against the dispatcher’s, and he has been able to speak directly to his mother. Neither fact guarantees her safety, and Nasir makes no such promise. He again offers a private conversation. Tuhin chooses to have Moin remain while he explains his fear; the first-person narration must respect that specific permission.

Tuhin says the boat did not sink. He saw Babul attacked. He asks for help contacting his family safely and giving a statement. The decision to speak is his, not a cure performed by the doctor.

**Chapter 18 — The account they can defend (22 December midday)**

The family put the human facts together: the pre-arranged sale, the afloat launch, the changing movement account, the actual injuries, and Tuhin’s direct observations. They give his allegation promptly to the escorts and investigating officer. They do not conduct a mock trial on deck.

Moin’s central deduction is: “আমরা ভেবেছিলাম নৌকাডুবির পরে লোকগুলো কী করেছে। আগে তো দেখতে হবে নৌকাডুবিটাই হয়েছে কি না।” The change in the question matters more than an ingenious gadget.

Babul remains the focus of the search and formal investigation. Knowing where the boat went does not reveal where a body has drifted. If recovered now, the recovery follows the authorised search, local knowledge and witness information—not a PhD tide calculation or drone image.

**Chapter 19 — A familiar skiff (22 December afternoon)**

Bashar returns in a familiar company skiff to retrieve papers and regain control of the situation. He has been asked ordinary questions previously and is not yet aware of everything Tuhin has disclosed. The authorities are being updated as the new account emerges; his arrival is not a deliberately arranged bait operation.

The skipper and escorts are already alert. Bashar stays at the boarding side rather than being granted free access to cabins. Tuhin is on the occupied working deck; nobody sends him out as bait or orders him to negotiate. Seeing him refuse to leave destroys Bashar’s expectation that the young employee can still be ordered about. The tension is social before it becomes physical: a familiar superior giving an order that is no longer obeyed.

**Chapter 20 — Stay with him (22 December, continuous scene)**

Bashar grabs for Tuhin at the boarding point and tries to pull him towards the skiff. The gap between boats and a shifting line make a few seconds dangerous. Jalal and deck crew control the vessels; an escort intervenes. Nasir brings Tuhin back towards the occupied deck and assesses him once safe.

Moin fetches help and keeps the passage clear. Fakhruddin gives a concise account of what he has just seen. Oli abandons the chance for close footage and stays where Nasir needs him. No teenager fights an armed gang, pursues a launch, or jumps into crocodile habitat.

The response is quick because the relevant people have been aboard since Chapter 6 and were alerted before this confrontation. This resolves a threat; it is not an instant conviction for Babul’s death.

**Chapter 21 — Evidence has owners (22 December evening)**

The responsible officers handle custody, interviews and preservation of the launch. Salek is dealt with through the shore investigation rather than materialising for a convenient final fight. Nasir does not question him as a condition of care.

Oli hands over originals or makes documented copies as requested. Mahbub provides ownership and maintenance papers; Hatem supplies his own transaction evidence; Tuhin gives his statement with appropriate support. Nasir’s clinical record preserves the distinction between observed injury and reported cause.

Moin sees only the portions he is present for. No chapter quietly enters a police interview room or private medical file that he cannot access.

**Chapter 22 — What happened to Babul (23 December, with a clearly marked later account)**

Explain the established immediate facts to the owner, crew and family: the launch was delivered afloat; a sale was arranged without consent; the sinking story is false; Tuhin has described an attack. Correct the earlier rumours in the same circles that heard them.

A separate, explicitly later passage supplies the completed findings. Babul’s recovery and examination, the verified records, the transaction trail and corroborated witness evidence establish the killing and each participant’s part. Salek’s invented voice story is withdrawn/exposed in that process. His genuine injury resulted from Babul resisting. A confession may corroborate the case, but it must not be the sole explanation dropped into the last chapter.

Return to Babul as a person. The family’s information matters because he and his family deserve a truthful account, not because the visitors need a second adventure credit.

**Chapter 23 — The second man in white (23 December night)**

The immediate human threat is contained. Joarrekha is at its approved station-side overnight anchorage while statements and next-day departure arrangements finish. The group do not seek another haunted creek.

Moin stands beside the real Nasir. Fakhruddin is close enough to share the view down a lit passage. A man with Nasir’s face, curly hair, panjabi and white payjama turns towards them, then passes the cabin corner. The real Nasir is still beside Moin. Crew and escort conduct a prompt, sensible check; nobody is found who accounts for the sighting.

Do not add an impossible footprint, stopped clock, dead battery, spectral photograph or message. Those would create additional mysteries. The clothing and calm turn are enough because the reader remembers Nasir’s entirely human boarding surprise.

Nothing about the human case changes.

**Chapter 24 — What I can tell you (24–25 December; later framing)**

Disembark, deal with ordinary travel, and let exhausted humour return carefully. The trip’s lost pleasures remain visible: an unused drone, a fishing plan cut short, the cook’s packed food for the road. Nasir still wears his panjabi and white payjama. Moin now associates that familiar sight with both safety and something he cannot place.

Fakhruddin returns to the US by 8 January, before the strict holiday deadline. The narrative may be written later, after the human case has reached its formal conclusion. That later perspective explains how Moin can report verified findings without having possessed police powers during the trip.

Suggested final thought:

> “বাবুল মাঝির সঙ্গে কী হয়েছিল, পরে আমরা জেনেছিলাম। কিন্তু নাসির ভাই আমার পাশে দাঁড়িয়ে থাকতে সামনে কাকে দেখেছিলাম—সেটা আজও বলতে পারি না।”

Do not finish by suggesting that jinn secretly caused the theft, influenced the verdict, or supplied the decisive clue.

**11. Pacing and causality checks**

The first five chapters establish why this particular family is together. Chapters 6–8 let the river holiday become worth losing. Chapters 9–15 move from care to uncertainty to a demonstrable falsehood. Chapters 16–21 narrow the case and contain the immediate threat. Chapters 22–24 account for the human harm and preserve the two unexplained experiences.

The investigation follows a chain of permissions and obligations: **guests become helpers; helpers notice contradictions; the owner asks legitimate questions about his own property; a witness chooses to speak; officials verify and act**. It never requires strangers to recognise amateur detectives.

Removing the paranormal events leaves a complete, intelligible crime story. Removing the cameras leaves a harder but still solvable case through the owner, yard, witness and records. Removing Nasir changes the story profoundly: the medical response, the early return and the witness’s confidence all depend on his actual skills and character. That is why he leads this sequel.

No one stays in a dangerous location merely to continue investigating. After the rescue, isolation decreases geographically while pressure increases socially: the threatened witness and dispatcher belong to the same working network. Keep most enquiries aboard and restrict shore scenes to the medical transfer, a lawful commercial landing, and official statements. The station-side overnight anchorage is forest-facing, reached around an approved bend, rather than a busy city quay; this is fictional placement, not an asserted real berth. The climax takes place near help already established, where a familiar man briefly becomes dangerous.

Moin must label later knowledge. Use phrases such as “তখন জানতাম না,” “পরে মামার কাছে শুনেছি,” and “মামলার পরে যে তথ্য জানা যায়…” only when they accurately describe the route by which he learned something. Do not use first-person narration to smuggle in omniscient scenes.
